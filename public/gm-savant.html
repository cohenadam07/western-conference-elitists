    Every front office, judged like a fund manager.

    GM Value Added is what a GM's teams did on the floor above an average GM in his seat, plus the value of the roster and picks he handed on, minus what he was handed. Points reward good teams; the goal is titles, so every GM also gets expected titles added, the same sum in titles, and a blend of the two you can tune. Every decision gets two grades: at the time, with only what was known that day, and how it turned out. Luck is kept apart, and so is shared credit.

    What's new (v1.0.2): players are matched by their seasons, not their names alone, so Jimmy Butler, J.R. Smith, Mike Dunleavy, Ron Harper Jr. and about 140 others now count as themselves: Butler's trades flip, Brad Stevens -640 GM Value Added, Leon Rose +906, Eddie Donovan -940. Frozen at-the-time grades were re-graded once to fix player matching, with old grades kept (5,742; most of them small, from an undrafted-rookie baseline the split careers had inflated).     Is it skill? The repeatability tests

    Each person's decisions are split in half five ways and the halves correlated across people. A skill repeats when the correlation's 95% interval stays above zero. "Smallest it can detect" is the smallest true correlation the test would find four times in five.
